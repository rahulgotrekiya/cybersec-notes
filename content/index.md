Working notes on cybersecurity, kept while studying and published here so they
stay useful outside my vault. Three tracks run through them: fundamentals and
network security, ethical hacking and penetration testing, and digital forensics
and incident response.

88 notes, written in Obsidian, published with Quartz. They are study notes rather
than a textbook — written to be re-read, so most are short and link heavily to
one another.

> [!tip] New here?
> Start with the [[00-Cyber Security MOC]]. It is the top-level map and links
> out to everything else in a sensible reading order.

## The maps

Each map of content is a hub for one track, ordering its notes so you are not
reading them alphabetically.

- [[00-Cyber Security MOC]] — the top-level map
- [[CSF & NS MOC]] — fundamentals and network security: CIA triad,
  segmentation, DMZ, protocols
- [[EHPT MOC]] — ethical hacking and penetration testing: recon, exploitation,
  post-exploitation
- [[DFIR MOC]] — digital forensics and incident response: acquisition,
  analysis, response
- [[Study Dashboard]] — what is finished and what is still in progress

## The sections

**[01-Concepts](01-Concepts)** — 29 notes, one idea each. The theory: what a
thing is and why it matters. Start at [[What is Cybersecurity]], then
[[CIA Triad]] and [[Networking Fundamentals]].

**[02-Quick-References](02-Quick-References)** — 12 cheatsheets built for
looking something up mid-task, like [[Nmap Commands Cheatsheet]],
[[Common Ports & Protocols Reference]] and [[OWASP Top 10 Quick Reference]].

**[03-Projects](03-Projects)** — 25 labs and practicals, each with its setup and
what actually happened. The capstones pull a whole track together:
[[Capstone - Full VAPT Report]] and [[Capstone - Full Forensic Investigation]].

**[04-Tools](04-Tools)** — 17 notes on individual tools: flags, worked examples
and the gotchas worth remembering.

> [!warning] Lab material
> Techniques here come from controlled lab environments, against machines I own
> or am authorised to test. Addresses in examples are RFC 1918 or textbook
> ranges. Use any of it only where you have permission.
